About me

Reading the world as a system. Communicating it with meaning.

I'm 25 and from the city of Santa Fe, Argentina. I study International Relations at the School of Political Science and International Relations of the National University of Rosario (UNR), and I specialised in digital marketing, political communication and paid media.

I run Carente de Sentido (“Lacking Meaning”), a communication agency I founded. The name is ironic and, at the same time, a manifesto: we live in a post-truth era, where algorithms let us build tailor-made realities that mix facts with fiction. My work is the opposite: communication with context and meaning.

International Relations taught me to look at events from a systemic perspective. That lens is a key tool for understanding social media, where everything moves faster and where much of public conversation now takes place.

I'm especially interested in international communication, both diplomatic and corporate, and in political marketing.

My view

The street used to be the rua. Today, the street is social media.

Social media, the new field of study

What used to be researched in the street is now read on social media: that's where trends, identities and public opinion take shape. Understanding it means understanding society.

A new dimension of foreign policy

Heads of state conducting foreign policy from their accounts, hybrid warfare, new tech players. Social media doesn't replace traditional channels, but it is now central to international relations.

A generation looking for a purpose

Many young people find fewer and fewer reasons to plan their future. I believe it's on us to approach institutions, introduce ourselves and say what we can contribute where we see ourselves growing.

Digital diplomacy

Diplomacy also happens in the feed.

An embassy no longer talks only to governments. It talks to citizens, students, businesses, journalists and the Argentine community abroad — and almost all of them get their information on a screen first.

01

Attention has moved

Press releases and protocol photos are still necessary, but no longer enough. Public conversation happens in short, visual formats with a human tone.

02

A country's image is built every day

Every post adds or subtracts. Culture, sport, science, food and tourism are entry points for a country to be chosen, visited and heard.

03

If we don't tell it, others will

In times of disinformation, a close and trustworthy official account is a diplomatic tool in itself, not an accessory.

Success story

Curators of Sweden: a country that handed its official account to its citizens

From 2011 to 2018, the Swedish Institute and VisitSweden handed the official @sweden account to a different citizen every week: teachers, students, farmers, professionals. With no institutional script, each one showed their own Sweden. The result was a diverse, close and credible national image.

8,000 → 66,500followers in the early years, as reported by the press
USD 40 Min media value, as estimated by the organisers
Grand PrixCannes Lions 2012, among other awards

The lesson isn't to copy the format but the idea: share the spotlight, show real people and speak to citizens in their own language.

Sources: Wikipedia, “Curators of Sweden”; The Week, “@Sweden's odd Twitter experiment”.

At an embassy

Specifically, I would…

01

Make social media dynamic and engaging

Agile content, with humour where appropriate and current formats such as reels, carousels and series, without losing institutional formality.

02

Bring the embassy closer to young people and citizens

Specific projects with schools, universities and youth organisations, built around key dates in the Argentine and host-country calendars.

03

Partner with people who drive reach

Well-known Argentines in the host country (sport, arts, science, food) who amplify messages and reach new audiences.

04

Run campaigns with clear goals

Different communication campaigns, each with its own objective: country image, culture, tourism, trade, education — all aimed at giving diplomacy a new kind of impact on citizens.

05

Serve the Argentine community abroad

Useful content that builds belonging for those living far away: cultural agenda, gatherings and news that keep them connected.

06

Make consular services clear

Procedures, appointments and notices explained in a simple, visual way, to reduce enquiries and improve the experience.

07

Communicate in more than one language

Content in Spanish and in the local language, to also reach the host country's public.

08

Measure, learn and report

KPIs, regular reports and a response protocol for crises or disinformation.
